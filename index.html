The Black BusinessResearch Table

Mapping Black-owned businesses in public records across the U.S.

Where do public programs list Black-owned businesses, and where are the gaps? Which industries do they work in, and how does that change over time?

The Research Table brings certification records from state, city and transportation programs into one panel, so researchers, journalists and policymakers can see both what is there and what is missing. Contact details are withheld from the public download and released to verified researchers on request.

Coverage & methods

What this database covers, and what it doesn't

Loading…

Coverage is defined by programs, not by where firms are based

One row per firm, built from listings

Programs publish listings, and many firms appear in more than one program or twice in one. The main file merges listings with the same normalized name and ZIP (or name, city and state where a program publishes no ZIP) into one firm and records which programs list it. Similar but different names are not merged; they are written to a review file. A firm that changed its name or ZIP can still appear twice. Every listing is kept in the listings file.

Certification is not existence

A lapsed certification shows that a program no longer lists a firm, not that the business closed. Formerly certified firms stay in the data, flagged in certification_status, and are left out of the headline count. A business that has been recorded is never removed.

Who this leaves out

Public certification records over-represent firms that sell to governments and under-represent informal, home-based and online-only businesses. States with no program here are gaps, not evidence that no Black-owned businesses exist there.

Why records are kept
Absence from a directory is never treated as a business closing, so a record that has been collected is not removed.
